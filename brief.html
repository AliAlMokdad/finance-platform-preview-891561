International Finance Learning Platform · Discussion note

From prototype to production

What you just saw runs entirely in the browser: one file, no installation, no data leaving the room. This note explains, in plain terms, what it would take to run it for real, and the three realistic ways to get there.

1 What the prototype already proves

Working today, no infrastructure
  • The full learning experience: languages including right to left, three learning styles (read and listen, play and confirm, watch), a visible journey through each procedure, interactive checks, points, certificate
  • Content verified verbatim against the finance SOPs and guidelines
  • Progress saved on the learner's device, resume on return
  • Works offline and on any modern browser, phone or laptop
Simulated today, real in production
  • Login and identity (today: a name field)
  • Progress visible to anyone but the learner (today: device only)
  • Manager notifications and certificate emails (today: shown, not sent)
  • Completion reporting for team leads and compliance
  • Source-document links behind each Reference

2 Three realistic paths

Inside Microsoft 365

Uses what the organisation already runs

Host the module on SharePoint, sign in automatically with the existing staff account, store progress in a SharePoint list, send manager notifications with Power Automate, surface the course in Teams or Viva Learning.

No new systems, no new vendor, data stays in the existing tenant, IT and GDPR posture already approved.

Reporting dashboards are functional rather than fancy.

Through an LMS

Standard e-learning packaging (SCORM or xAPI)

Package each module in the standard e-learning format and load it into a learning management system, which then owns enrolment, tracking, reminders and certificates.

Mature tracking and compliance reporting out of the box.

Depends on which LMS the organisation has or chooses; the custom look and feel is partly constrained by the player.

Standalone web app

A small dedicated service

Keep the experience exactly as the prototype and add a small backend for accounts, progress and email, hosted on infrastructure the organisation controls.

In practice this would be built on a standard modern web stack (for example Next.js with Tailwind and Framer Motion for the interface, plus a small managed database), which any in-house team or agency can maintain.

Total design freedom, the prototype is already most of the front end.

Something new to host, patch and own; needs an explicit data protection review.

The honest comparison: the prototype's front end survives in all three paths. The choice is only about where identity, progress and notifications live. Nothing built so far is wasted.

3 Data protection, answered early

  • The prototype sends nothing anywhere: names, emails and progress stay in the learner's browser and can be wiped in one click.
  • In production, learner records are personal data: they belong on organisation-controlled infrastructure, under the existing GDPR framework, never on private or third-party consumer services.
  • No AI service is involved in the learning content. Everything shown is authored and verified against the source documents in advance.

One thing worth deciding early: who owns the content. The platform is data driven, so a finance team member can update a module by editing text, not code. That keeps the SOPs and the learning in step, which is the entire point.
